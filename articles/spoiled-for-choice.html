Spoiled for Choice. The Choice Was One.

Victor Romanov · 13 August 2026 · Discussion on LinkedIn

Key facts

  • Task: a large tour operator ran all its IT from one data center. The critical half had to move to a second site, and both sites had to run as one metrocluster.
  • Main limit: dark fiber reach of about 30 km by cable route, not by the straight line on a map.
  • Result: out of dozens of data centers, weighted criteria left one site at about 18 km of fiber.

A large tour operator ran its entire business from a single data center.
Bookings, payments, the lot.
One hall. One power chain. One very bad day when the engineering plant failed and everything went dark for hours.

For a travel company, dark means customers cannot book and money stops moving.
The fix was not "find a better data center."
It was "never stand on one leg again."

So the system gets split across two sites.
The most critical half moves to a new, stronger data center.
The two halves run as one metrocluster.
One live system across two data centers, kept in sync over dark fiber.
Now a single failure cannot take the company offline.

Which turned an easy question into a hard one.
Where does the critical half go?

The metro area lists dozens of data centers.
On paper, spoiled for choice.
Then the requirements walk in.

The link between the sites rides dark fiber.
And dark fiber is measured by cable route, not by the straight line on a map.
Every duct and splice eats optical budget.
Past roughly 30 km of route, the light gets too weak for a link you can trust.

That one rule did most of the cutting.
Three sites made the shortlist.

A metrocluster rides two diverse fiber routes, so a single cut cannot split it.
The winning site came back at 17.6 and 31.8 km on its two routes.
The short one fits the budget with room to spare. The long one is already scraping it.
The other two sites had no route that fit (42 and 43 km).

Distance matters. But it is only the first cut.
The real scorecard ran deep, every criterion ranked by weight.
These are the ones that did the cutting:

Tier III, concurrently maintainable.
12+ kW per rack, with headroom.
Two carriers on-net, sub-5 ms, 99.98% SLA.
ISO 27001 and PCI DSS.
A clean incident record, references included.
Contiguous space for six racks.

And a dozen more, ranked below these.

One site cleared every line. Roughly 18 km of fiber.
Not the biggest name. The only one that held.

This is the quiet truth of infrastructure.
You are never spoiled for choice.
The constraints choose.

You just read them in the right order ...
